Redacted docs

Redacted launches coins as pairs. One click deploys a token on pump.fun and inscribes its twin on Monero. You trade on pump.fun with everyone else. When you want your bag off the public ledger, you burn the pump.fun tokens and the same amount is credited to you on Monero, one for one.

Monero has no token layer and no smart contracts, the same as Bitcoin. The Monero side uses exactly the trick Ordinals used on BTC: the coin and every bridge are written into transactions, and an indexer reads the chain back to agree on who holds what.

The pair

Every Redacted coin exists in two places at once, with one supply shared between them.

pump.fun · Solana

An SPL token on pump.fun's bonding curve. This is where price is found, where liquidity lives, and where anyone can buy or sell with SOL. It graduates to PumpSwap at 85 SOL like any other pump.fun coin.

Monero · inscribed

The twin. Same name, same ticker, same 1,000,000,000 supply. Balances are held against holder keys recorded in Monero transactions. It starts at zero and only grows by burns on the Solana side.

The lock is simple arithmetic: tokens on pump.fun plus tokens on Monero always equals one billion. A token moves to Monero only by being burned on Solana, so the Monero side can never hold more than has been destroyed on the public side. Nothing is minted that was not burned.

The bridge is one way by design. Monero is where bags go to stop being watched. Anyone who wants back onto the public ledger sells on pump.fun before bridging, not after.

The tech, start to finish

Redacted is pump.fun's launch model with a Monero twin attached. Pump.fun proved the shape: one curve for every coin, no presale, and when the curve fills the liquidity moves to a DEX and the LP is burned so nobody can pull it. Redacted keeps every step as pump.fun runs it and adds a second chain the tokens can retire to.

Pump.fun, piece by piece

On Solana a pump.fun coin is an SPL token. The bonding curve is a program that holds SOL on one side and tokens on the other. Every buy and sell is an instruction to that program. At 85 SOL the program migrates the SOL and the reserved tokens into PumpSwap and burns the LP tokens. The burn is the whole point: once the pool exists nobody, not the dev and not pump.fun, can remove the liquidity, and the curve can never trade again.

One coin, start to finish

  1. Launch. You give a name, ticker, picture and description. Redacted creates the pump.fun coin and, in the same breath, sends a Monero transaction carrying the launch inscription: the pump.fun mint address, the metadata hash and your holder key. The two are now paired.
  2. Trade. Buys and sells happen on pump.fun's curve with the same 30 SOL and 1,073,000,000 token virtual reserves every pump.fun coin starts with. Redacted reads the curve and shows the chart, the holders and the progress.
  3. Burn. You send pump.fun tokens to the burn address with a memo holding your Monero holder key. Those tokens are gone from Solana for good.
  4. Credit. The indexer sees the burn confirm, writes a bridge inscription to Monero for the same amount, and credits your holder key. Your bag is now on Monero, one for one, with no sender, receiver or amount visible on-chain.
  5. Order. Solana order decides which burns count and when. Monero block order decides the inscription record. Any indexer replaying both chains reaches the same balances.
  6. Graduate. At 85 SOL pump.fun migrates the liquidity to PumpSwap and burns the LP. Redacted moves the coin to On PumpSwap. Bridging keeps working after graduation, because burning a PumpSwap token is the same as burning a curve token.
  7. Hold. Tokens on Monero sit against your holder key. Transfer between keys is a signed inscription. They never go back to Solana.

Why the burn matters

Pump.fun's LP burn makes graduation irreversible: nobody can pull the pool. Redacted's bridge burn makes privacy irreversible in the same way: the tokens that reach Monero were destroyed on the public side, so the public ledger can only ever show that something left, never where it went or how much is held by whom. Two burns, two one-way doors, and the supply stays exactly one billion across both.

What ships today: real wallets, real pump.fun curves read from Solana, real trades through PumpPortal, launches through the launchpad backend with creator fees to the launchpad wallet, and bridge requests the launchpad fulfils by hand. The inscription format and the indexer described here are the design the automated Monero side runs on.

Inscribed like Ordinals

Ordinals put data on Bitcoin without changing Bitcoin: an inscription rides inside a normal transaction, and anyone running an indexer reads the chain in order and agrees on what exists. BRC-20 turned that into tokens with three operations, deploy, mint and transfer, interpreted off-chain.

Redacted does the same thing on Monero. Each action is a small inscription in a Monero transaction, and the launchpad is the indexer.

InscriptionWhat it carriesOrdinals equivalent
launchThe pump.fun mint it pairs with, metadata hash, the dev's holder key.deploy
bridgeCoin id, Solana burn signature, amount, receiving holder key.mint
transferCoin id, amount, from key, to key. Signed by the sender's key.transfer

Order is decided by the chain, not by the server. Two indexers replaying the same blocks reach the same balances, exactly as two Ordinals indexers agree on the same inscriptions. Monero adds one thing Bitcoin never had: the sender, the receiver and the amount of every inscription are hidden by default.

Launching a coin

  1. Connect a wallet. Phantom, Solflare, Backpack or any injected Solana wallet. Redacted never holds keys. Everything is signed in the wallet.
  2. Name, ticker, picture. Two to thirty-two characters for the name, two to ten letters or digits for the ticker, and an image up to 4 MB. pump.fun requires the image.
  3. Description and links. One sentence, up to 280 characters. Website, X and Telegram are optional and must be real https links.
  4. Dev buy. Optional, in SOL. Executed as the first buy on the curve, so the whole board can see what the dev holds.
  5. Sign one transfer. Your wallet sends 0.7 SOL plus your dev buy to the launchpad wallet. That is the only thing you sign.
  6. The launchpad creates the coin. The backend creates it on pump.fun from the launchpad wallet, runs your dev buy and sends you the tokens. The coin appears under New with its mint.

Why the launchpad wallet is the creator

pump.fun pays a share of every trade to the wallet that created the coin, for the life of the coin. Redacted creates every coin from one wallet, so every creator fee on every coin comes to the launchpad. That revenue is what pays for the Monero side: the indexer, the inscriptions, and the manual swaps until the indexer ships. The dev gets the coin and the dev buy, not the fee stream.

Supply is 1,000,000,000 on pump.fun. 793,100,000 sell on the curve, 206,900,000 seed PumpSwap at graduation. The Monero side starts at zero and fills only by burns. A second mode, wallet mode, lets the connected wallet create the coin directly through PumpPortal for testing. In that mode the creator is the user, so the fees go to them. It is off by default.

Trading

Trading is pump.fun's. Open any coin and Redacted reads its bonding curve straight from Solana: virtual reserves, real reserves, completion flag and creator, decoded from the curve account. The trade box quotes from that state before you commit:

  • Tokens or SOL you receive, and the share of supply that represents.
  • pump.fun's 1% fee in SOL.
  • Price impact against the current spot price, plus the slippage and priority fee the transaction will carry.

Pressing buy or sell asks PumpPortal for a transaction. Redacted appends one more instruction to it, the 3% transfer to the launchpad wallet, so the fee and the trade are one signature and one block. Your wallet signs it, Redacted sends it to the RPC and waits for confirmation. After graduation the same box routes to PumpSwap. Quick buttons fill 0.1, 0.5, 1 SOL or your balance on the buy side, and 25, 50, 75 or 100 percent of your bag on the sell side. The holders table is the top twenty token accounts on chain with owners resolved, refreshed every time you open the coin, and the price polls every twelve seconds while the page is open.

Any existing pump.fun coin can be tracked by pasting its mint in the bar above the board. Redacted reads its metadata and curve and lists it like a native launch.

Bridging

Below the trade box on every coin page is the bridge. It shows your pump.fun balance on the left, your Monero holder key on the right, and an amount field.

  1. Pick an amount of your pump.fun bag. 25, 50 and 100 percent buttons are there.
  2. Request. The request carries the mint, the amount, your wallet and your holder key to the launchpad.
  3. Burn. The launchpad replies with the burn instructions. You send the tokens to the burn address with your holder key in the memo. They are gone from Solana for good.
  4. Receive. Once the burn confirms, the same number of tokens is inscribed to your holder key on Monero.

Until the indexer ships, the launchpad confirms burns and writes inscriptions by hand, funded by the creator fees. There is no platform fee on the bridge. The only cost is the Monero network fee for the inscription. The bridge works before and after graduation. If the bridge endpoint is not configured yet, requests are kept in your browser so nothing is lost.

Graduation

When pump.fun's curve has collected 85 SOL of real deposits, or the last curve token sells, pump.fun migrates the SOL and the 206,900,000 reserved tokens into a PumpSwap pool and burns the LP tokens. From that moment the coin trades on PumpSwap and the board moves it to On PumpSwap.

Nothing changes on the Monero side. Balances already bridged stay where they are, and tokens bought on PumpSwap can still be burned and bridged exactly as before. On the ledger the coin moves from Ascending to Ascended.

Wallet

Connect wallet in the header opens a picker for Phantom, Solflare, Backpack or any injected Solana wallet. Detected wallets connect in one click; the rest link to their install page. Once connected the pill shows your address and SOL balance, and reconnects on your next visit if the wallet allows it.

Redacted never sees a private key. Three things ever ask for a signature: the SOL transfer at launch, and each buy or sell. Every one of them is a transaction you can read in the wallet before approving.

Alongside the Solana wallet, Redacted keeps a Monero holder key for you in the browser. It is the key your bridged tokens are credited to. Click the wallet pill to see it. Back it up: if the browser storage is cleared, the key is gone.

Integration

The front end is static. Everything that needs a private key or a database runs in a backend you host, wired in through config.js. Fill in launchpadWallet and the three endpoints below.

POST api.launch

Multipart form: image, name, symbol, description, website, twitter, telegram, devBuySol, buyer (the user's wallet), transferSig (the signature of the SOL transfer to the launchpad wallet), holderKey. The backend verifies the transfer landed, creates the coin on pump.fun from the launchpad wallet, executes the dev buy, sends the tokens to buyer, and replies { "mint": "...", "signature": "..." }.

GET api.coins

Returns the coins this launchpad created, newest first: [{ mint, name, symbol, image, description, createdAt, creator }]. The front end merges it with the browser's own list every minute, so visitors see the same board.

POST api.bridge

JSON: { mint, amount, holderKey, wallet, at }. The backend queues it, replies { "ok": true, "inscription": "..." } or burn instructions, confirms the burn on Solana, and writes the inscription on Monero.

Everything else in config.js

  • rpc: Solana RPC endpoints, tried in order. Put a keyed one first.
  • pumpPortal, slippage, priorityFee: how buy and sell transactions are built.
  • createMode: backend (fees to the launchpad) or wallet (testing only, fees to the user).
  • launchCostSol and platformFeePct: the 0.7 SOL birth cost and the 3% trade fee. Both go to launchpadWallet.

Safety

Redacted moves real SOL. Read every transaction in your wallet before you approve it. The launch transfer goes to the launchpad wallet shown in the config line under the stats; if that line says the config is missing, nothing can be launched and nothing should be sent.

Assume the ordinary outcome for launchpad coins: most go to zero. The bridge is one way, so anything you move to Monero can only be sold on Monero. Never put in funds you are not fine losing, and never trust a launchpad that hides the curve.
